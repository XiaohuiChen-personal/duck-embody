Who should read this / what you'll learn. This doc specifies the simulated apartment that the Duck Embody experiment runs in: a small multi-room home, built entirely from code, that an LLM must explore through a robot's eyes. It assumes no robotics or Isaac Sim background — every term of art (USD, collider, PhysX, SimReady, and so on) is defined on first use. After reading you will know the exact floor plan and dimensions, where every asset comes from (with verified URLs), how the scene is assembled and collision-proofed, what can go wrong at 0.4× scale, and how we validate the scene before any LLM ever sees it.

1. Goals and constraints

The experiment (see Doc 01) drops the Open Duck Mini v2 — a 42 cm bipedal robot — into an apartment it has never seen, and asks an LLM to find the kitchen using only camera frames and tool calls. There is no classical SLAM (Simultaneous Localization and Mapping — the standard robotics approach of building a geometric map from sensor data with dedicated algorithms); the LLM writes its own map as text. That puts four hard requirements on the scene itself:

  1. Multi-room and unknown. The apartment must have several visually distinct rooms with doorways, so that "find the kitchen" requires actual exploration, room recognition, and route memory — not a single glance.
  2. Visually recognizable from a 0.36 m camera. The robot's head camera sits about 36 cm off the floor. Each room must be identifiable by a vision-language model (VLM — a model that answers questions about images) from that viewpoint: a kitchen must look like a kitchen, a bedroom like a bedroom.
  3. Reliable collision. Walls and furniture must physically stop the robot. A wall the robot can ghost through invalidates bump reporting, path length, and the whole navigation task. Collision behavior must be verified per asset, not assumed.
  4. Reproducible from code. The build machine (DGX Spark) is headless — there is no GUI for hand-placing furniture, and hand-authored scenes cannot be diffed or code-reviewed. The entire scene must be generated from a Python data structure, so a fresh clone plus one asset-fetch script rebuilds the identical apartment.

Terminology used throughout: USD (Universal Scene Description) is the file format Isaac Sim uses for 3D scenes — a hierarchical scene graph of prims (nodes: meshes, transforms, lights). PhysX is NVIDIA's physics engine inside Isaac Sim. A collider is the invisible physics shape attached to a prim that makes it solid; PhysX cooks (preprocesses) colliders once at scene load.

2. The duck-scale decision (0.4×)

Locked: the apartment is built at 0.4× human scale ("duck scale"), rather than placing the duck in a full-size home. Why: the duck's eye height of ~0.36 m becomes the equivalent of ~0.9 m in a real home (0.36 / 0.4 = 0.9) — a low but workable human vantage — instead of a toddler's view where counters loom overhead and no room is readable.

Two failure modes drive this. First, readability: in a full-scale home a 36 cm camera sees table undersides, sofa skirts, and cabinet kickboards. Countertops — the very target of the task — would be invisible. At 0.4×, a scaled counter top sits ~0.36 m high, right at eye level, and room-defining objects (fridge, bed, sofa) fill the frame the way they do for a standing person at ~0.9 m.

Second, traversal time: the locomotion policy's command hull caps forward speed at 0.222 m/s, and motion macros drive at vx = 0.2 m/s. Crossing a real 12 m apartment would burn the 240 policy-second budget on pure transit. At 0.4× the apartment is 4.8 × 3.6 m; the longest oracle route (bedroom spawn, seed 102, to the kitchen counter: ~4.0 m via doorway centers, a little more with furniture-clearing detours) is roughly 20–25 s of driving, leaving the budget for exploration and recovery. source: command hull and caps, Doc 01; straight-line segment sums spawn→door centers→target from the §3.1 coordinates (seed 102: 0.56 + 1.50  + 1.95 = 4.01 m; seeds 101/103/104 are shorter). The definitive per-seed oracle lengths come from scoring.py's shortest-path code once it exists (§4)

All furniture USDs are spawned with a uniform scale=(0.4, 0.4, 0.4); walls and doorways are authored directly at duck scale. Human-equivalent numbers (÷0.4) are given in the dimensions table so sanity checks stay easy.

3. Floor plan and dimensions

Three rooms in a row along the south side, one full-width hallway along the north, four doorways. The east–west coordinate is x (0 at the west wall), north–south is y (0 at the south wall); headings are degrees counter-clockwise from +x (so 90° faces north / +y). The bedroom is reachable only via the hallway; the living room and kitchen are additionally connected directly, giving the map a loop — useful for testing the LLM's adjacency reasoning.

Wall thickness (0.03 m) is exaggerated in the drawing for visibility. Furniture footprints are the planned 0.4×-scaled sizes, provisional until inspect_assets.py reports measured bounding boxes (§8).

4. apartment_layout.py — the single source of truth

Locked: one Python dict in duck_embody/env/apartment_layout.py is simultaneously the scene specification (the scene builder reads it to place walls and furniture) and the scoring ground truth (the scorer reads room polygons, doorway adjacency, target region, and spawn points from it — and from nothing else). Why: if the scene and the answer key are two files, they drift; one dict makes "the map the robot walked through" and "the map it is graded against" provably identical.

The file currently exists as a docstring stub source: duck_embody/env/apartment_layout.py (1 line, checked 2026-07-26); this section is its design. Annotated schema:

# apartment_layout.py — THE layout. Scene builder AND scorer import LAYOUT; nothing else
# defines geometry. All lengths in meters at duck scale; headings in degrees CCW from +x.

LAYOUT = {
    "units": "m",
    "world_scale": 0.4,            # uniform scale applied to every furniture USD at spawn
    "extents": (4.8, 3.6),         # apartment footprint (x, y)
    "wall_height": 0.5,
    "wall_thickness": 0.03,

    # Walls: straight segments; door gaps are encoded by SPLITTING a wall into
    # segments, so the builder never subtracts geometry. Each segment -> one CuboidCfg.
    "walls": [
        {"name": "A1", "start": (0.0, 2.7),  "end": (0.725, 2.7)},   # wall A west of living door
        {"name": "A2", "start": (1.075, 2.7),"end": (2.375, 2.7)},
        # ... A3, A4, B1, B2, C, plus 4 outer walls
    ],

    # Rooms: axis-aligned polygons. SCORING ground truth for room precision/recall,
    # "which room is the robot in", and the post-episode layout QA.
    "rooms": {
        "living_room": {"poly": [(0.0, 0.0), (1.8, 0.0), (1.8, 2.7), (0.0, 2.7)]},
        "kitchen":     {"poly": [(1.8, 0.0), (3.3, 0.0), (3.3, 2.7), (1.8, 2.7)]},
        "bedroom":     {"poly": [(3.3, 0.0), (4.8, 0.0), (4.8, 2.7), (3.3, 2.7)]},
        "hallway":     {"poly": [(0.0, 2.7), (4.8, 2.7), (4.8, 3.6), (0.0, 3.6)]},
    },

    # Doorways: ground-truth adjacency edges (for map-accuracy scoring) + gap geometry
    # (consumed by the wall-splitting builder and the oracle shortest-path computation).
    "doorways": [
        {"between": ("hallway", "living_room"), "center": (0.9, 2.7),  "width": 0.35},
        {"between": ("hallway", "kitchen"),     "center": (2.55, 2.7), "width": 0.35},
        {"between": ("hallway", "bedroom"),     "center": (4.05, 2.7), "width": 0.35},
        {"between": ("living_room", "kitchen"), "center": (1.8, 1.2),  "width": 0.35},
    ],

    # Furniture: scene spec only (scoring never reads it). "collision" declares how the
    # asset becomes solid: "native" (collider ships in the USD) or "bbox_proxy"
    # (invisible cuboid sized from inspect_assets.py output — §6, §8).
    "furniture": [
        {"name": "sofa",    "room": "living_room", "asset": "simready/crestwood_sofa",
         "pos": (0.35, 1.6), "yaw_deg": 90, "collision": "native"},
        {"name": "counter", "room": "kitchen", "asset": "isaac/sektion_cabinet",
         "pos": (2.55, 0.22), "yaw_deg": 90, "collision": "native", "count": 2},
        {"name": "fridge",  "room": "kitchen", "asset": "archvis/Appliances/<pick>",
         "pos": (3.05, 2.4), "yaw_deg": 180, "collision": "bbox_proxy"},
        {"name": "bed",     "room": "bedroom", "asset": "archvis/Furniture/Beds/<pick>",
         "pos": (4.15, 0.75), "yaw_deg": 90, "collision": "bbox_proxy"},
        # ... coffee table, armchair, stool, desk, plant (SimReady, native)
    ],

    # Target: success = base within `radius` of `point` + declare_done (Doc 01).
    # SEMANTICS PINNED: this disc IS "the kitchen-counter target region" — the scorer
    # measures Euclidean distance to `point`, never to the cabinet footprint. A robot
    # pressed against the counter face at (2.2, 0.5) is 0.43 m from `point` -> FAIL.
    # That exact case is a required fixture in tests/test_scoring.py (pre-batch gate).
    "target": {"name": "kitchen_counter", "room": "kitchen",
               "point": (2.55, 0.75), "radius": 0.35},

    # Spawns: seed -> pose. Seeds are the trial IDs; identical across models.
    "spawn_points": {
        101: {"pos": (0.5, 0.5),  "heading_deg": 90},
        102: {"pos": (4.3, 2.2),  "heading_deg": 270},
        103: {"pos": (0.4, 3.15), "heading_deg": 0},
        104: {"pos": (1.4, 2.3),  "heading_deg": 180},
    },
}

Downstream consumers: scene_builder.py (walls + furniture → Isaac Lab scene config, §6), scoring.py (rooms, doorways, target, spawns → success, SPL oracle path, map precision/recall, drift-at-goal), and tests/test_layout.py (invariants: rooms tile the extents without overlap, every doorway lies on a shared room boundary and matches a wall gap, spawns and target are inside their rooms and off furniture footprints, and every spawn keeps ≥0.4 m clearance from walls and furniture AABBs — added after design review found the original west-end plant ~0.17 m from spawn 103 and the original armchair pose ~0.23 m from spawn 101; both items were relocated, §3.1).

ADDED IN T2.4 — doorway centre-line corridors, and the armchair moved again (0.95 → 0.72). The invariants above have a blind spot that a scoring bug fell straight through. The armchair sat 0.40 m to one side of the living-room/kitchen doorway, so it passed "nothing sits in a doorway" (0.40 > the 0.30 m clearance test), and A* still found a route, so "every room is reachable from every spawn" passed too. But its body-inflated footprint reached y = 1.2225, leaving roughly 7 cm of free centre-line inside a doorway whose robot corridor is y 1.105–1.295.

That is not a driving problem, it is a fairness problem: the SPL oracle would have routed through that 7 cm slot, and every model that sensibly detoured via the hallway would be scored against a threading the robot cannot reliably walk. Reachability tests answer "is there a path?"; the new test_doorway_approach_corridors_are_clear answers the question that actually matters — "is the path the oracle scores against one the robot can walk?" — by requiring the full ±0.5 m centre-line through every doorway to be free. At y = 0.72 the corridor clears by 0.113 m and the chair is off the rug. The moved scene was re-rendered and the T2.3 recognition gate re-run: still 4/4 rooms in all 3 repeats. evidence: tests/test_layout.py (mutation-checked against the old pose), results/figures/survey/judge_result.json

5. Asset sourcing

All furniture comes from NVIDIA's public Omniverse content bucket, reachable anonymously over HTTPS. The Isaac-version-pinned tree root is https://omniverse-content-production.s3-us-west-2.amazonaws.com/Assets/Isaac/5.1 — an S3 prefix, not a HEAD-able object (HEAD on the bare root returns 404); the tree is verified via HTTP 200 on object keys beneath it plus a list-type=2 listing source: §10, checks 1–5, 2026-07-26. Bucket listing shows the other two catalogs are siblings of that tree, not children: SimReady props live under Assets/simready_content/ and ArchVis under Assets/ArchVis/Residential/ source: S3 list-type=2 on prefix Assets/, 2026-07-26 (§10, checks 4–5).

SimReady is NVIDIA's "simulation-ready" asset standard: USDs that ship with colliders and semantic labels (machine-readable class tags like sofa) already authored. ArchVis ("architectural visualization") assets are render-quality models with no physics at all — pretty, but the robot walks through them unless we add physics ourselves.

CORRECTION (T0.2, 2026-07-26) — SimReady colliders are OFF by default. This doc originally stated that SimReady props ship with colliders included. Measured against the mirrored assets, that is only half true: the colliders exist, but behind a USD variant set named PhysicsVariant whose default selection is None. As spawned out of the box, every SimReady prop has zero UsdPhysics.CollisionAPI prims — precisely the walk-straight-through failure §7 warns about, and one that collision_props cannot repair.
  • Fix: the builder passes UsdFileCfg(variants={"PhysicsVariant": "RigidBody"}) (Isaac Lab supports variant selection at spawn from_files_cfg.py:104). Selecting it yields convexDecomposition colliders (armchair: 3 prims; measured).
  • …plus static: the only other option is RigidBody, which also applies UsdPhysics.RigidBodyAPI — furniture would become dynamic and a leaning duck could shove the sofa across the room. The builder therefore also sets rigid_props=RigidBodyPropertiesCfg(kinematic_enabled=True): solid, immovable obstacles, which is what the navigation task needs.
  • One genuine catalog defect: desk_01 carries the variant set but ships zero collision prims under either option (each was selected and counted). It is reclassified bbox_proxy in assets/asset_list.tsv.
scripts/inspect_assets.py applies each asset's variants before measuring, so manifest.json describes the asset as it will actually be spawned, and a declared-vs-measured mismatch is a hard error.
CORRECTION (T0.2, 2026-07-26) — ArchVis is authored in CENTIMETRES. Measured metersPerUnit: SimReady and Isaac Props = 1.0 (metres), every ArchVis asset = 0.01. Isaac Lab's stage is metres and does not auto-convert layer units, so the scale=(0.4, 0.4, 0.4) hardcoded in §6.4's sketch would spawn a 1.87 m fridge as a 187 m one. §7 anticipated this risk ("the builder computes the effective scale"); this records the measured numbers. The correct factor is effective_scale = metersPerUnit × 0.4 — i.e. 0.4 for SimReady/Isaac Props and 0.004 for ArchVis — and manifest.json publishes it per asset as scale_for_duck_scale, so the builder never recomputes it.
Locked: assets/fetch_assets.sh mirrors every referenced USD (plus textures) into assets/ and records SHA-256 checksums; the scene builder resolves paths only against the local mirror. Why: the experiment batch must never depend on a live bucket — a mid-batch network hiccup or upstream asset change would silently break cross-model comparability.
License boundaries. NVIDIA's official ready-made Environments (Office, Hospital, etc.) are under a "Limited Use" no-modification license — they may not be remixed into a custom apartment, so we build our own from props. Separately, the ArchVis EULA has not yet been read against our plan to publish rendered GIFs of episodes in the public repo — tracked as an open question (§11). MolmoSpaces (fallback, §9) is CC BY 4.0 and unproblematic.

6. Scene builder mechanics

duck_embody/env/scene_builder.py translates LAYOUT into Isaac Lab scene-config entries. Isaac Lab is configuration-driven: you describe each scene object as a config dataclass, and the framework spawns it at startup. Three mechanisms, all verified against Isaac Lab 2.3.2 sources:

6.1 Walls: CuboidCfg with native collision

Each wall segment becomes an AssetBaseCfg whose spawner is a CuboidCfg (a box primitive) with collision_props set. For shape primitives Isaac Lab defines (creates) the collision schema on the prim — box colliders need no mesh cooking and are the most reliable collision geometry PhysX has. source: ~/IsaacLab/source/isaaclab/isaaclab/sim/spawners/shapes/shapes.py:295-297 (define_collision_properties when cfg.collision_props is set); CuboidCfg at shapes_cfg.py:62. Walls are 0.03 m thick — comfortably above PhysX contact tolerances, so a walking robot cannot tunnel through at 0.2 m/s.

6.2 Furniture: AssetBaseCfg + UsdFileCfg at fixed poses

Static furniture follows the pattern Isaac Lab itself uses for the table in its stacking task: an AssetBaseCfg with an initial pose and a UsdFileCfg spawner source: ~/IsaacLab/source/isaaclab_tasks/.../manipulation/stack/stack_env_cfg.py:41-44. We add scale=(0.4, 0.4, 0.4) and semantic_tags=[("class", name)] (supported field source: .../sim/spawners/spawner_cfg.py:41) so debug tooling can query what the camera sees.

Why scaling is safe here: Isaac Lab applies scale=cfg.scale inside create_prim at the moment the USD reference is added to the stage — i.e., the scale is authored on the prim before simulation starts and before PhysX parses the stage, so colliders are cooked at the scaled size rather than rescaled afterwards. source: ~/IsaacLab/source/isaaclab/isaaclab/sim/spawners/from_files/from_files.py:314-319 (_spawn_from_usd_file, def at :268). Uniform scale only — non-uniform scale on mesh/convex colliders is a known PhysX trouble spot and buys us nothing here.

6.3 Collider proxies for visual-only assets

ArchVis assets get a co-located invisible CuboidCfg (visible=False, collision_props set) sized from the asset's axis-aligned bounding box (AABB — the smallest axis-aligned box containing the mesh), computed offline by inspect_assets.py (§8) and recorded in assets/manifest.json. The visual USD spawns with no collision config; the proxy is the physics.

6.4 Scene assembly

Everything lives under one /World/Apartment scope with num_envs=1 (a single environment — no vectorized cloning, unlike training). Every scene asset sets collision_group=-1: Isaac Lab collects such prims into the global collision group, which is what makes them collide with the robot rather than only within their own environment copy. source: .../assets/asset_base_cfg.py:69 (collision_group: Literal[0, -1] = 0); .../scene/interactive_scene.py:754, 802 (global prim path collection when collision_group == -1).

# scene_builder.py — shape of the generated config (sketch)
def wall_asset(seg: dict, L: dict) -> AssetBaseCfg:
    cx, cy, length, yaw = segment_pose(seg)          # midpoint + orientation from LAYOUT
    return AssetBaseCfg(
        prim_path=f"/World/Apartment/walls/{seg['name']}",
        init_state=AssetBaseCfg.InitialStateCfg(pos=(cx, cy, L["wall_height"] / 2),
                                                rot=yaw_to_quat(yaw)),
        spawn=sim_utils.CuboidCfg(
            size=(length, L["wall_thickness"], L["wall_height"]),
            collision_props=sim_utils.CollisionPropertiesCfg(),   # DEFINES a box collider
            visual_material=sim_utils.PreviewSurfaceCfg(diffuse_color=WALL_COLOR),
        ),
        collision_group=-1,
    )

def furniture_asset(item: dict) -> AssetBaseCfg:
    return AssetBaseCfg(
        prim_path=f"/World/Apartment/furniture/{item['name']}",
        init_state=AssetBaseCfg.InitialStateCfg(pos=(*item["pos"], 0.0),
                                                rot=yaw_to_quat(item["yaw_deg"])),
        spawn=sim_utils.UsdFileCfg(
            usd_path=str(ASSETS_DIR / MANIFEST[item["asset"]]["local_path"]),  # mirror only
            # PER-ASSET scale, NOT a hardcoded 0.4: metersPerUnit x duck_scale.
            # 0.4 for SimReady/Isaac Props (metres), 0.004 for ArchVis
            # (centimetres). Authored pre-PhysX-parse (§6.2). See the units
            # correction box in §5.
            scale=(s, s, s) if (s := MANIFEST[item["asset"]]["scale_for_duck_scale"]) else None,
            # SimReady colliders live behind PhysicsVariant, default "None" (§5).
            variants=MANIFEST[item["asset"]]["variants"] or None,
            # ...and RigidBody would make furniture dynamic, so pin it static.
            rigid_props=sim_utils.RigidBodyPropertiesCfg(kinematic_enabled=True)
                        if item["collision"] == "native" else None,
            semantic_tags=[("class", item["name"])],
            collision_props=OFFSET_OVERRIDE if item["collision"] == "native" else None,
        ),
        collision_group=-1,
    )
    # if item["collision"] == "bbox_proxy": emit an additional invisible CuboidCfg
    # sized from MANIFEST[item["asset"]]["size_m_at_duck_scale"] at the same pose
    # (§6.3). Note flat assets (blue_rug measures 0.002 m tall) need either a
    # minimum proxy thickness or — better, for a rug — no proxy at all: a rug is
    # not an obstacle.

7. Risks and mitigations

RiskMechanismMitigation
Contact offsets don't scale PhysX's contactOffset/restOffset (distances at which contacts are generated/come to rest) are authored in ABSOLUTE meters and do not shrink with the 0.4× scale. A 2 cm contact offset authored for a human-scale table becomes an invisible force field around a 20 cm-tall duck-scale table — the robot gets shoved before visually touching anything. inspect_assets.py reads authored offsets offline via pxr (§8); the builder overrides them on every native-collision asset via CollisionPropertiesCfg(contact_offset=0.002, rest_offset=0.0), applied nested to all collider prims source: modify path at from_files.py:333-334 → schemas.py:366. Exact value tuned in the physics pass (§8.3).
collision_props cannot create colliders UsdFileCfg(collision_props=...) only MODIFIES colliders that already exist in the USD: the schema helper returns False if the prim has no UsdPhysics.CollisionAPI source: ~/IsaacLab/source/isaaclab/isaaclab/sim/schemas/schemas.py:400-402. A visual-only asset spawned this way renders perfectly and the robot walks straight through it — no error, no warning. Per-asset verification: inspect_assets.py counts collision-API prims in every mirrored USD; assets declaring "native" in the layout must have ≥1 or the build fails; everything else is forced to "bbox_proxy". The scripted physics pass (§8.3) then bump-tests each furniture item.
MDL gray-frame warmup MDL (NVIDIA's material definition language) materials compile on first use; the first rendered frames come out flat gray/black. A gray first observation would poison the LLM's very first room guess. The session runner performs N warmup renders (N determined empirically in the survey render, starting at 8) after scene load, before any captured frame; render_scene_survey.py verifies frame N is fully shaded.
Stage-unit mismatch USD files declare metersPerUnit; some catalogs author in centimeters (0.01). A cm-authored asset dropped in unaware would be 100× too large, and 0.4× of wrong is still wrong. inspect_assets.py records metersPerUnit and the measured AABB per asset; the builder computes the effective scale so every asset lands at its intended duck-scale footprint, and the survey render (§8.2) catches anything that slips through visually.
Camera sees over walls Walls are 0.5 m; the camera sits at 0.36 m with a wide FOV, so upper frame regions can glimpse adjacent rooms at distance, possibly confusing room identification. HAPPENED, and the contingency was taken (T2.3). The judge named the hallway from furniture visible over the 0.5 m walls. Walls raised to 0.7 m — necessary but not sufficient; a ceiling was also required (see the gate-result box below §8).

8. Validation loop design

Three stages, cheapest first. Only stage 2 launches Isaac Sim (the "kit" process — startup costs minutes and only one GPU job may run at a time), and it launches it exactly once.

8.1 inspect_assets.py — offline, no kit

Pure-pxr (the standalone USD Python library) pass over every mirrored USD. Records per asset: metersPerUnit, default prim, AABB via UsdGeom.BBoxCache, count of prims with UsdPhysics.CollisionAPI, and any authored contactOffset/restOffset. Output assets/manifest.json is the input for proxy sizing (§6.3) and the native-collision gate (§7). Runs in seconds, no GPU.

8.2 render_scene_survey.py — one kit launch

Builds the full scene once and captures: (a) a top-down orthographic view (no perspective — directly comparable against the §3 floor plan), and (b) a duck-height (0.36 m) four-heading sweep at each room's center — exactly what look_around() will produce. Acceptance: a VLM, shown each room's sweep frames cold, names the room correctly for all four rooms; plus visual checks (no floating/sunken furniture, doorways visibly open, materials shaded after warmup). Survey renders are committed to results/figures/ as evidence.

8.3 Scripted no-LLM physics pass

Same kit session, scripted controller driving the real locomotion policy (no LLM): traverse every doorway in both directions, deliberately bump the sofa (SimReady native collider), a wall (CuboidCfg), and the fridge (bbox proxy). Assert: colliders hold (no penetration beyond contact offset), the harness reports bumps, doorways pass without snagging, and nothing terminates the episode except a genuine fall. This is the gate that catches both walk-through furniture and invisible-force-field offsets before any scored trial.

GATE RESULT (T2.3, 2026-07-26) — the scene needed two structural changes to pass, both driven by judge evidence.

1. Walls raised 0.5 m → 0.7 m. §7's "camera sees over walls" row named this exact contingency, and the gate produced exactly that evidence. At 0.5 m the out-of-benchmark judge described the hallway as an "outdoor courtyard/pathway with red lounge furniture" and as "a mattress with a pillow lying on the ground" — it was naming the hallway from the living-room sofa and the bedroom bed visible over the walls. Raising to 0.7 m fixed the bedroom (2/3 → 3/3) but not the hallway.

2. A ceiling was added, with per-room lights. Not in the original design. Even at 0.7 m the judge kept calling rooms "an outdoor terrace/rooftop courtyard" — open sky above a wall does not look like a home, and the question the gate asks is literally "what room of a home is this?". Sealing the rooms also makes them dark, so a sphere light per room arrives with the ceiling; the two are inseparable. The ceiling is visual only (the duck cannot fly) and is hidden for the top-down render, so §3.1's "low enough for top-down debug renders" still holds — the survey script toggles its visibility rather than forcing a choice between the two.

3. Kitchen and hallway got more of their own furniture. The kitchen failed twice ("a mostly empty white room with a chair and a cabinet" → living room) because all its kitchen-ness sat in one low run along the south wall, invisible from the middle and north of the room: added a second cabinet run along the east wall and a microwave on the counter. The hallway was the least stable room of all, so it gained two planters along its length.

Instrument change: 5 poses per room, not 3, and the gate must pass 3 runs. No locked model supports deterministic decoding (doc 05 §7.1), so one pass of the gate is a sample rather than a verdict — measured: with 3 poses the hallway scored 3/3, 2/3 and a 1/1/1 tie across three runs on identical frames. A scoring bug was fixed at the same time: Counter.most_common breaks ties by insertion order, so a 1/1/1 split silently "passed" whichever guess arrived first, and the gate reported 4/4 on an unrecognisable room. A tie is now explicitly not a majority.

Final result: 4/4 rooms in every one of 3 runs — bedroom 5/5, hallway 5/5 & 4/5 & 4/5, kitchen 4/5 in all three, living_room 4/5 & 5/5 & 5/5. evidence: results/figures/survey/judge_result.json, judge_result_run*.json, and the 80 committed survey frames

9. Fallback ladder

If the primitive-walls-plus-props apartment fails its acceptance checks (rooms not recognizable, collision unfixable), we fall back in order — each rung trades some experimental cleanliness for robustness:

  1. MolmoSpaces (AllenAI). USD exports of iTHOR/ProcTHOR interactive home scenes, explicitly targeting Isaac Sim 5.1.0 + Isaac Lab 2.3.1 (we run 5.1.0/2.3.2), CC BY 4.0, plain download script. Known caveat to document if used: their collision-group setup needs adjusting to collide with an externally added robot (same global-group issue as §6.4). Scaled 0.4×, their human-scale walls become ~1 m — taller than our 0.5 m spec, an acceptable relaxation.
  2. InteriorAgent (SpatialVerse / Manycore Tech; listed among Isaac Sim's third-party SimReady assets). Photoreal interior scenes; custom "InteriorAgent License", and its publisher states testing only against Isaac Sim 4.2/4.5 (we run 5.1.0) — both make it a last-resort research option, not a default. source: Hugging Face dataset card spatialverse/InteriorAgent ("Tested with: Isaac Sim v4.2, Isaac Sim v4.5"; "released under InteriorAgent License"), checked 2026-07-26
  3. Labeled-cuboid floor. Colored primitive boxes with text signage ("KITCHEN"). Always works, zero asset risk — but we expect (an expectation, not a cited result) that a VLM cannot recognize a "kitchen" in bare geometric proxies, so the task degrades toward signage OCR; before adopting this rung, that expectation gets verified with a quick VLM probe on proxy-only renders. This rung changes what the experiment measures and would be reported as such.

11. Open questions

  • ArchVis EULA vs. published media. May rendered GIFs/frames containing ArchVis assets be published in the public repo README? Needs an actual license read before any media lands in results/figures/ destined for publication.
  • Per-asset physics of the final ArchVis picks. "Zero physics tokens" was verified on one downloaded sofa (Arnold.usd); the specific bed/fridge/oven files chosen for the scene must each pass inspect_assets.py before the native-vs-proxy decision is final.
  • Sektion_Cabinet articulation. The cabinet has doors/drawers (it is used as a manipulation asset elsewhere). Does spawning it as a static AssetBaseCfg leave the doors rigidly fixed, or must joints be explicitly locked? Resolved in the survey render + physics pass.
  • Wall height 0.5 m vs. over-wall visibility. Whether the wide-FOV camera glimpsing adjacent rooms over 0.5 m walls helps or hurts VLM room identification — decided by the §8.2 acceptance run; contingency is 0.7 m walls.
  • Contact-offset override value. 2 mm proposed (§7); the right value is whatever makes the §8.3 bump tests report contact at visually plausible distances without jitter.
  • Furniture poses and spawn clearances are provisional until inspect_assets.py returns measured 0.4× footprints; positions in §3/§4 assume estimated bounding boxes and may shift by a few centimeters (layout dict is the only place they change).
